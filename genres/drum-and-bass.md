# Genre: Drum & Bass

## Overview

Drum & Bass is characterized by fast breakbeats (170–180 BPM), heavy sub-bass, and dramatic
contrasts between rolling grooves and explosive drops. Lighting should match this intensity
arc — restrained and atmospheric during breakdowns, building through rolls, then unleashing
everything on the drop. DnB has the most extreme dynamic range of any EDM genre, and lighting
must exploit this — silence and darkness punctuated by massive intensity is the LD's primary weapon.

## BPM Range

- Typical: 170–180 BPM
- Common: 174 BPM (1 beat = 344.8ms, 1 bar = 1379.3ms)
- Time signature: 4/4 (but feels double-time due to breakbeat patterns)
- Snare hits on beats 2 and 4 — the rhythmic anchor for all lighting syncs

## Energy Profile

DnB has the most extreme contrast between sections of any EDM genre. Drops are peak energy,
breakdowns can be near-silent. The lighting should mirror this dynamic range.

| Section | Energy | Duration | Lighting Character |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------------|
| Intro | Low–Medium | 8–16 bars | Atmospheric wash, minimal movement, establish palette |
| Build | Medium–High | 8–16 bars | Ramp intensity, add fixtures, accelerate strobe/movement |
| Drop | Peak | 16–32 bars | FULL SEND — all fixtures, fast movement, strobes, lasers |
| Rolling Bass | High | 8–16 bars | Sustained energy, rhythmic chases, less chaotic than drop; can have multiple rolling sections at different energy levels |
| False Drop / Fake Out | Medium | 1–4 bars | Blackout or dramatic strip-down that mimics a drop, then quickly resolves or drops hard — confuses the audience for maximum impact when real drop hits |
| Breakdown | Low | 4–16 bars | Strip to minimal — single color wash, slow sweep, near-dark; can be melodic (vocal, piano) or dark (sub-bass rumble) — adjust lighting warmth accordingly |
| Second Build | Medium–High | 8–16 bars | Rebuild tension, layer effects progressively, often faster than first build to create urgency |
| Second Drop | Peak+ | 16–32 bars | Even bigger than first — different palette, more effects, all reserves spent |

## Color Palette

### Primary Colors (use most often)

- **Deep Blue** (0, 0, 255) / BSW Blue (44) / Sharpy Blue (30) — the signature DnB color, cold and aggressive, works across all subgenres (liquid, neurofunk, jump-up). Most universally used color in DnB.
- **Red** (255, 0, 0) / BSW Red (20) / Sharpy Red (10) — high energy, danger, intensity, essential for contrast with blues
- **White** (255, 255, 255) / BSW White (0) / Sharpy White (0) — primarily used for strobes and strobe bursts rather than sustained looks; critical impact weapon on drops

### Accent Colors (use for contrast and variety)

- **Purple** (128, 0, 255) / Sharpy Purple (50) — dark, moody, good for breakdowns and melodic sections
- **Cyan** (0, 255, 255) / BSW Teal (56) / Sharpy Teal (70) — cold but lighter than blue, excellent for rolling bass sections and mid-energy sustained looks
- **Amber** (255, 128, 0) / Sharpy Amber (80) — warmth for melodic breakdowns (vocal, piano), strong contrast with blues
- **Orange** / BSW Orange (26) / Profile Orange (25) — similar to amber, useful for neurofunk builds and dark melodic tension
- **Green** (0, 255, 0) / BSW Green (38) / Sharpy Green (40) — rare in DnB but can work for liquid drum and bass sections; avoid as primary color

### Subgenre-Specific Notes

- **Liquid Drum & Bass**: Warm palette (amber, orange, white), slower breakdowns (8–12 bars), emphasis on smooth mover sweeps and atmospheric texture
- **Neurofunk / Technical**: Cold palette (deep blue, purple, cyan), minimal warm tones, may use UV/blacklight effects, tighter beats, aggressive gobo/prism use
- **Jump-Up / Clownstep**: Bright, chaotic palette (cyan, red, white), rapid position changes, heavy par chasing, fun rather than dark aesthetic
- **Liquid Funk**: Hybrid of liquid smoothness and funk energy — Blue + Amber blend, medium-fast builds, emphasis on rhythmic precision

### Two-Color Pairs (primary busking combos)

| Combo Name | Color A | Color B | Best For |
|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| Classic DnB | Deep Blue | Red | Drops, high energy across all subgenres |
| Ice | Deep Blue | White | Clean drops, strobe emphasis, neurofunk |
| Neon Night | Purple | Cyan | Rolling bass, mid-energy, liquid sections |
| Fire & Ice | Red | Cyan | Builds, hard contrast moments, jump-up |
| Midnight | Purple | Deep Blue | Breakdowns, dark atmosphere, neurofunk |
| Blood Moon | Red | Amber | Dark melodic breakdowns, aggressive drops |
| Whiteout | White | Red | Maximum impact drops, emergency peaks |
| Deep Liquid | Cyan | Amber | Smooth rolling, liquid builds and breakdowns |

### Avoid

- Pastels and warm tones as primary palette (too soft for DnB energy)
- Green as a primary color (reads as "nature" rather than "aggressive") — exception: liquid DnB secondary accent
- Yellow as a primary color (too cheerful for neurofunk, works only in jump-up)

## Movement Conventions

### Drop Movement

- **Fast mover sweeps**: L→R or R→L across 2–4 beats, timed to downbeats
- **Split movers**: Sharpy and BSW crossing to opposite sides on the drop hit
- **Profile punching**: Downstage on snare accents (beats 2 and 4)
- **NI3K head rotation**: Maximum speed continuous motion, higher movement values (160–250 tilt range for rotation)
- **Position changes**: Every 1–2 beats during peak energy
- **Crossover patterns**: Movers passing through each other mid-drop for visual chaos

### Rolling Bass Movement

- **Smooth 2–4 bar sweeps**: Movers flowing rather than snapping between positions
- **Figure-8 and circular patterns**: Movers tracing ballyhoo paths timed to bassline rhythm; figure-8 works well for rolling sections with strong sub-bass
- **Gentle left-right oscillation**: Side-to-side motion at 2–3 bar periodicity
- **NI3K slow rotation**: Creating ambient texture without distraction
- **Synchronized to bass rhythm**: If rolling bass has a distinct rhythmic pattern, match movement pace to bassline frequency (typically 1/2 note or 1/4 note frequency)

### Breakdown Movement

- **Static or very slow drift**: 8+ bar sweeps, minimal position changes
- **Movers converge**: To center, upstage (if lifting), or ceiling hit for atmospheric isolation
- **Minimal position changes**: Let the stillness contrast with the drops — a single slow position holds for entire breakdown
- **Optional slow prism rotation**: Only on movers; silent beam effect, no gobo

### Build Movement

- **Accelerating sweep speed**: Start at 4-bar smooth, end at 1-beat snaps by drop
- **Movers gradually spreading**: From center to wide stage positions
- **NI3K ramping**: From static to slow rotation to fast rotation into drop
- **Snare-synchronized accents**: Sharp mover positions on beats 2 and 4 (snare hits)

## Strobe Conventions

- **On drops**: White or single-color strobe, 1/8 or 1/16 note speed, 2–4 bars maximum (never entire drop)
- **On builds**: Accelerating strobe (start slow, increase to fast over 4–8 bars), creates tension toward drop
- **Blackout + strobe**: Brief blackout (1–2 beats) followed by strobe hit for maximum impact
- **Never**: Sustained strobe through an entire section — it loses impact and causes audience fatigue
- **Fixture strobes**: Use Sharpy ch6 strobe (64–120) and BSW ch16 strobe (20–128) for beam-only strobes while pars hold steady; creates depth and separates beam layer from par wash
- **White strobe emphasis**: Most DnB strobes use white or single-color intensity strobes (not gobo strobes); reserve gobo strobes for builds and rolling sections only

## Laser Conventions

- **Drops only**: NI3K lasers on during drops, off during everything else (except build reveal)
- **Build reveal**: Bring lasers in at the last 2–4 bars of a build for anticipation; creates "something coming" feeling
- **Color**: Match (red laser on red palette) or contrast (green laser on blue palette) with mover color palette
- **Strobe lasers**: Use for the biggest moments only — laser strobe during the peak 2–4 bars of drop 2, maximum 2–4 bars
- **Laser rotation**: NI3K can rotate lasers during drop for chaos effect (coordinate with head rotation)
- **Safety**: Laser always off during breakdowns and rolling sections to avoid audience fatigue

## Gobo & Prism Conventions

- **Drops**: Tight gobos + prism spinning (fast rotation 128–189 or 194–255) creates chaotic, energetic beams; gobo wheel cycling (stepping through G1, G2, G3) on beat changes
- **Rolling**: Open gobo (clean beam) with optional slow prism rotation (spinning 128–189 at slow rate); maintains energy without chaos
- **Breakdowns**: Open gobo, no prism, no gobo rotation — clean and simple aesthetic; beam simplicity mirrors lighting restraint
- **Builds**: Add gobo partway through (4–8 bars before drop), add prism in final 2–4 bars before drop; progressive complexity increase

## Par Behavior

- **Drops**: `par_wash()` all same color for maximum unity and visual coherence, or `par_pairs()` alternating with beat (P1+P3 vs P2+P4) for rhythm-driven energy
- **Rolling**: `par_gradient()` for subtle depth, or `par_wash()` at reduced intensity if tracking with mover color
- **Breakdowns**: `par_wash()` at low intensity (30–50% dimmer), single cool or warm color depending on breakdown character (dark = blue, melodic = amber)
- **Builds**: Progressive `par_chase()` speeding up with build energy, or pars lighting one at a time in synchronized pairs; final 4 bars before drop: full `par_wash()` at high intensity
- **Par + Kick Sidechain**: Pars can "pump" or "breathe" with kick drum sidechain compression effect — dimmer oscillates 20% on downbeats, creates tactile bass feel without strobes

## Fixture Layering

Effective DnB shows layer fixture types strategically to create depth and control energy peaks.

### By Section Type

**Drops**
- **Beams** (Sharpy + BSW): Fast movement, strobes, gobo/prism effects — aerial element, chaos layer
- **Spots** (Profile): Downstage accents on snare hits (beats 2 and 4), gobo texture, prism spins — rhythmic precision layer
- **Pars** (4BAR + Missyees): Wash color layer holding steady or pulsing with beat, high intensity
- **Audience Blinder** (NI3K): Occasional intense hits or strobe, last 1–2 bars only for maximum shock

**Rolling Bass**
- **Beams**: Smooth figure-8 movement, open or minimal gobo, slow prism rotation
- **Spots**: Slower position changes (4-bar drifts), gobo texture held steady
- **Pars**: Gradient pattern or wash in same color as beams, reduced intensity (70–80%)
- **Audience Blinder**: Off or very dim halo glow only

**Breakdowns**
- **Beams**: Static or very slow 8+ bar drift, center or ceiling, open gobo, no prism
- **Spots**: Off or minimal color accent (optional single gobo wash)
- **Pars**: Minimal intensity (20–40%), single cool or warm color
- **Audience Blinder**: Off (darkness is restraint)

**Builds**
- **Beams**: Accelerating movement speed, add gobo in final 8 bars, add prism in final 4 bars
- **Spots**: Gentle upstage/downstage movement, introduce gobo partway through
- **Pars**: Progressive chase or stepped lighting, final 4 bars full wash at high intensity
- **Audience Blinder**: Halo color glow starting 8 bars before drop, increasing intensity

### Layering Principle

Each section should have a **primary layer** (the audience's main focus), **secondary layer** (texture and motion), and **accent layer** (occasional impacts).

- **Drops**: Primary = beams (fast chaos), Secondary = pars (wash color), Accent = spots (snare hits) + NI3K (peak moments)
- **Rolling**: Primary = beams (smooth motion), Secondary = pars (gradient texture), Accent = spots (gentle gobo)
- **Breakdowns**: Primary = pars (minimal wash), Secondary = beams (slow drift or off), Accent = none (restraint)
- **Builds**: Primary = beams (accelerating), Secondary = pars (chase pattern), Accent = spots + NI3K (final moments)

## Haze & Atmosphere

Haze density dramatically affects how beams and strobes read. DnB lighting requires strategic haze to maximize beam visibility during high-intensity moments without obscuring visibility during fast movements.

### Haze Density by Section

| Section | Density | Purpose |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Intro | Light (20–30%) | Establish atmosphere, minimal beam visibility required |
| Build | Medium (40–60%) | Ramp with intensity, beams become increasingly visible |
| Drop | Heavy (70–85%) | Maximum beam visibility, strobes cut through, chaos effect |
| Rolling | Medium–Heavy (60–75%) | Sustain visibility, smooth movement looks painterly |
| False Drop | Heavy (80%+) | Blackout haze hit creates disorientation, then clears slightly for real drop |
| Breakdown | None–Light (0–15%) | Darkness, minimal beam effect, cold sparse look |
| Second Build | Heavy (70–80%) | Aggressive build, almost drop-density haze |
| Second Drop | Maximum (85%+) | Thicker than first drop for "bigger" feeling |

### Haze + Lighting Interaction

- **Strobes in haze**: White strobes cut through heavy haze (70%+); colored strobes wash out in haze, use only in light haze (20–40%)
- **Beam visibility**: Requires 50%+ haze to be visible; at 0% haze, beams are nearly invisible
- **Par wash**: Works at any haze density, but warm colors (amber, orange) read better in heavy haze; cool colors (blue) need 50%+ haze to stand out
- **Gobo projection**: Requires 60%+ haze to be legible; at 30% haze, gobo patterns dissolve
- **Audience view**: Heavy haze (80%+) on stage creates spectacle but impairs audience ability to see performers; use sparingly (5–10 bars per section)

## Venue Scale Notes

DnB lighting conventions vary significantly between club (intimate, 1,000–5,000 cap) and festival (large-scale, 20,000+ cap) environments.

### Club Scale (1–5k attendees)

- **Fixture count**: 8–20 movers, 4–8 wash fixtures, 2–4 strobes
- **Movement speed**: Faster movements (1–2 beat sweeps) feel more impactful in smaller space
- **Beam precision**: Tighter gobos and cleaner prism work; audience is close, details visible
- **Haze**: Lighter haze (20–60%) — heavy haze obscures performers on small stage
- **Color intensity**: Saturated, vibrant colors read better; pastels wash out
- **Strobes**: Use strategically (1–3 bars max) to avoid overwhelming small crowd
- **Par behavior**: `par_wash()` preferred over `par_chase()` — chasing looks frantic in tight spaces
- **Audience blinder**: NI3K used sparingly; direct laser hits across audience create intimacy but risk safety issues

### Festival Scale (20k+ attendees)

- **Fixture count**: 100+ movers, 50+ pars, 20+ strobes (Rampage benchmark: 260+ movers, 110+ strobes)
- **Movement speed**: Larger, slower sweeps (4–8 bar movements) feel proportional to enormous stage
- **Beam precision**: Loose gobos, heavy prism effects acceptable; audience distance means fine details dissolve anyway
- **Haze**: Heavier haze (70–85%) required for beam visibility across large distance
- **Color intensity**: Even saturated colors fade over distance; use high contrast pairs (blue + white, red + cyan)
- **Strobes**: Extended strobe sequences (4–8 bars) appropriate; larger crowd tolerates and expects more intensity
- **Par behavior**: `par_chase()` and `par_gradient()` work well; rhythmic chasing reads from far away
- **Audience blinder**: Full-power NI3K hits acceptable for peak moments; lasers visible across entire crowd
- **Laser shows**: Complex laser geometries and multiple laser sources create spectacle; single laser reads poorly at festival scale

### Scaling Approach

When adapting a show from club to festival scale:
1. Keep color palette identical
2. **Double** movement durations (2-bar sweep → 4-bar sweep)
3. **Halve** strobes density (every beat → every 2 beats) but extend duration (2 bars → 4 bars)
4. Increase haze density by 20–25%
5. Add parallel movers or spread movement across more fixtures to fill larger stage
6. Strengthen contrast between sections (darker breakdowns, brighter drops)

## Notable References

Real-world DnB lighting exemplars that embody the genre's philosophy:

### LDs & Production Designers

- **Ed Warren** — Master of DnB restraint and timing. Philosophy: "Hit the beat, the off beat. Wait for the last possible moment. Save something for the next drop." Every effect earned, never wasted.
- **Nomad** — Liquid drum & bass specialist. Known for atmospheric, smooth mover work and warm color palettes in breakdowns; precise snare-hit timing on spots.
- **Logistics & High Contrast** — Neurofunk champions. Aesthetic: tighter, faster gobos, heavy purple/blue palettes, minimal color changes, prism-heavy movement.

### Venues & Festivals

- **Rampage Festival** — DnB flagship (UK, annual). Benchmark: 260+ movers (Sharpy, MAC Aura), 110+ strobes, full laser array. Lighting density showcases maximum DnB potential; use as reference for 20k+ capacity.
- **Let It Roll** — Czech DnB mega-event. Known for cohesive color direction across entire event (typically deep blue + red palette), excellent breakdown/build contrast.
- **Fabric (London)** — Legendary club intimate venue. Lighting proves that DnB impact comes from **timing and restraint**, not quantity — 15 movers can destroy with proper beat-syncing.

### Shows & Events Worth Studying

- **Netsky @ Fabric** — Liquid DnB; smooth movement, warm breakdown palettes, excellent rolling bass rhythm syncing
- **High Contrast @ Let It Roll** — Neurofunk; tight gobo work, consistent color palette, aggressive builds
- **Sub Focus @ festivals** — Hybrid liquid-tech; combines smooth rolling sections with explosive drops, varied color palettes
