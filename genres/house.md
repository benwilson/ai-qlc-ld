# Genre: House

## Overview

House music encompasses a wide range from deep and minimal to progressive and tech. The
unifying thread is a steady four-on-the-floor kick at 120–130 BPM with groove-based energy
that builds gradually rather than dropping explosively. Lighting should feel warm, flowing,
and rhythmic — enhancing the groove rather than overwhelming it.

House is **fundamentally warm**. The genre's lighting DNA descends directly from disco's
"dancefloor glow" — a honeyed amber/gold ambiance where the audience becomes part of the light
show. The LD is a "lighting DJ," reading the crowd and building energy smoothly through
crossfades, not shock effects. Every beat should feel like the room is breathing with the
production's sidechain compression.

## BPM Range

- Typical: 120–130 BPM
- Common: 124–128 BPM
- Time signature: 4/4 (four-on-the-floor kick)
- Reference timing: 126 BPM = 1 beat at 476.2ms, 1 bar at 1904.8ms

## Energy Profile

House has a more gradual energy curve than bass music genres. Transitions between sections
are smoother, and the "drop" is often more of an **arrival** than an explosion.

| Section | Energy | Duration | Lighting Character |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------------|
| Intro | Low | 8–16 bars | Warm amber/gold wash, minimal movement, establish groove |
| Groove Build | Low–Medium | 8–16 bars | Gentle evolution, add layers one at a time, par pulse breathing |
| Melodic Rise | Medium | 8–16 bars | Colors deepen, movers widen, add texture, maintain warmth |
| Peak/Arrival | High | 16–32 bars | Full warm palette, sweeping movers, smooth and joyful, breathing intensifies |
| Emotional Break | Medium | 8–16 bars | Strip back, intimate feel, single warm color family |
| Final Build | Medium–High | 8–16 bars | Layer everything back, build to climax with warm tones |
| Outro | Low | 8–16 bars | Return to opening feel, gentle fade to amber |

## Color Palette

### Primary Colors
- **Disco Gold** (255, 160, 40) — THE house signature, warm honeyed glow, disco heritage
- **Warm White** (255, 200, 120) — candlelight softness, inviting club atmosphere, foundation
- **Deep Blue** (0, 0, 255) / BSW Blue — late night, deep house moods, used sparingly
- **Magenta** (255, 0, 255) / BSW Magenta — vibrant, peak moments only (not everyday)

### Accent Colors
- **Amber** (255, 128, 0) / Sharpy Amber — warmth, golden hour, soul, pairs well with gold
- **Teal** (0, 255, 128) / BSW Teal / Sharpy Teal — fresh, deep, sophisticated
- **Pink** (255, 64, 128) / BSW Pink / Sharpy Pink — playful, melodic moments
- **Cyan** (0, 255, 255) — clean, modern, tech house (cooler subgenre accent)

### Two-Color Pairs
| Combo Name | Color A | Color B | Best For |
|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| Disco Fire | Disco Gold | Amber | Warm peaks, core house energy |
| Sunset | Amber | Magenta | Warm peaks, melodic house (magenta accent only) |
| Deep Ocean | Deep Blue | Teal | Deep house grooves, late night |
| Club Classic | Magenta | Disco Gold | Disco-inspired peak moments |
| Golden Hour | Warm White | Disco Gold | Opening, grooves, intimate moments |
| Cool Dawn | Cyan | Blue | Tech house, minimal (cooler subgenre only) |
| Neon Rose | Pink | Magenta | Melodic peaks, emotional (sparse use) |
| Night Sky | Deep Blue | Teal | Atmospheric breakdowns |

### Avoid
- Harsh red as a primary (too aggressive for house energy)
- Cool colors as the foundation (house needs warmth)
- Rapid color snaps (house is about flow, not shock)
- Monochrome white/red/green (too "rock concert" or "industrial")
- Over-reliance on magenta (save for peaks only)

## Movement Conventions

### Peak Movement
- Smooth 2–4 bar sweeps, never jarring snaps
- Figure-8 or wide arc patterns on movers
- Gentle spread from center to wide positions
- Movers should feel like they're dancing, not fighting
- Slow continuous movement that feels organic to the groove

### Groove Sections
- Slow, continuous drift (8+ bar movements)
- Subtle left-right oscillation matching the groove
- Movers staying relatively close to center
- Movement driven by the bassline, not the kick
- Think "floating in space," not "tracking targets"

### Breakdown Movement
- Converge movers to center or DJ booth
- Slow, intimate — like a spotlight on the moment
- Optional: ceiling hit for ethereal break with haze
- Can hold a single position for 8+ bars in deep house

### Build Movement
- Gradual spread from center to wide (over 8–16 bars)
- Smooth, never accelerating dramatically
- Color evolution rather than position changes
- Speed increases as energy builds (but never snappy)

## Timing Presets

House favors longer timing presets than other genres. 4-bar and 8-bar smooth fades are the
bread and butter. At 126 BPM: 1 beat = 476.2ms, 1 bar = 1904.8ms.

| Preset | FadeIn (ms) | Hold (ms) | Use |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-----|
| House Smooth 2bar | 3810 | 0 | Smooth color transitions (2 bars at 126 BPM) |
| House Smooth 4bar | 7620 | 0 | Extended smooth sweeps (bread and butter) |
| House Smooth 8bar | 15240 | 0 | Slow atmospheric fades (grooves and breakdowns) |
| House Breathe (4bar) | 7620 | 7620 | Sidechain pulse mimicking kick compression (2 fade + 2 hold) |
| House Breathe (8bar) | 15240 | 15240 | Slow breathing for minimal sections |
| House Beat | 0 | 476 | Beat-synced accents (1 beat at 126 BPM) |
| House 2Beat | 0 | 952 | 2-beat hold for subtle chase |
| House Bar | 476 | 1428 | Soft snap per bar (1 beat fade + 3 beat hold) |
| House Strobe | 0 | 125 | 1/4 beat strobe (use sparingly, magenta or cyan preferred) |

## Strobe Conventions

- **Sparingly**: House is not a strobe-heavy genre — reserve for the biggest peak moments
- **Short bursts**: 2–4 beats maximum, then back to smooth
- **Color strobe**: Magenta or cyan strobe feels more "house" than white
- **Par flash**: Use par strobe (ch4 or ch2) for subtle rhythmic flash rather than full rig strobe
- **Never**: Sustained strobe through an entire section
- **Breathing strobe**: Use quick 1-beat flashes (not sustained strobe) on pars to mimic sidechain compression

## Laser Conventions

- **Peaks only**: Lasers on during the biggest peak moments, 4–8 bars maximum
- **Subtle**: Single laser color matching the palette, not all-three blast
- **Accent**: Brief laser reveal at the top of a build for a "wow" moment
- **No strobe**: Laser strobe feels too aggressive for house — steady on or off
- **Warm lasers**: Red or amber laser preferred over green/blue for house aesthetic

## Gobo & Prism Conventions

- **Peaks**: Open gobo with optional single prism for texture
- **Grooves**: Open gobo, clean beams, focus on color and position
- **Breakdowns**: Open gobo, no prism — clean and minimal
- **Optional**: Slow gobo rotation during extended breakdown for subtle texture
- **Disco tradition**: Mirror ball gobos (if available) are house's heritage — use for opening/closing

## Par Behavior

Par fixtures are THE FOUNDATION of house lighting (60–70% of visual impact). Movers provide
accents; pars create the ambient glow.

- **Peaks**: `par_wash()` in warm, unified color (Disco Gold or Warm White) for maximum cohesion
- **Grooves**: `par_gradient()` for depth and sophistication, warm tones throughout
- **Breakdowns**: `par_wash()` at medium intensity, warm tones (Disco Gold or Amber)
- **Builds**: `par_gradient()` evolving through color families, starting warm, staying warm
- **Breathing pulse**: Subtle 1-beat brightness pulses on pars matching the kick (mimics sidechain)
- **Never strobe pars hard**: Use smooth intensity fades, not flashing

## Fixture Layering

House lighting is built in layers. Understand the role of each fixture type:

### Foundation (60–70% of visual)
**Pars and Wash Fixtures** — The warm ambient glow that defines house. These carry the mood
and establish the warm aesthetic. Par color should match the song's energy and moment.
- Opening: Disco Gold or Warm White at 70–80% intensity
- Grooves: Warm washes with gentle intensity breathing (subtle sidechain)
- Peaks: Bright, unified colors (Disco Gold, Warm White, or Magenta)
- Breakdowns: Intimate warm tones at 50–60% intensity

### Texture Layer (20–25% of visual)
**Movers (Sharpies, BSW, Profiles)** — Positioned to create depth and guide eye movement.
Movers are accents that follow the groove, not leads.
- Role: Slow sweeps, gentle oscillation, position changes at 2–4 bar intervals
- Energy: Light, elegant, never aggressive
- Color: Match the par palette or provide subtle contrast
- Frequency: ~1 position change per 4–8 bars in grooves, more frequent at peaks

### Accent Layer (5–10% of visual)
**Lasers and Specials (NI3K, gobos, prisms)** — Sparse, high-impact moments.
- Lasers: Peak moments only, 4–8 bars max
- Gobos/Prisms: Optional texture during extended breakdowns
- Keep usage minimal — house's power comes from restraint, not effect density

### DO NOT (Inverse of Bass Music)
- Do NOT lead with movers (leads to chaotic eye movement)
- Do NOT use beams as the primary visual (opposite of DnB/dubstep)
- Do NOT layer multiple effect types simultaneously
- Do NOT treat pars as an afterthought

## Haze & Atmosphere

House clubs use haze strategically — enough to make beams visible, but not so heavy that it
obscures the room's warm glow.

### Haze Level
- **Target**: Medium haze (30–40% density)
- **Effect**: Movers' beams are visible and textured, but the room remains clear and warm
- **Avoid**: Heavy wall of smoke (too industrial, kills the warm ambient feel)

### Haze Timing
- **Grooves**: Light haze, subtle beam texture
- **Peaks**: Increase haze density slightly for beam definition
- **Breakdowns**: Reduce haze or fade to clear (intimate feel)
- **Transitions**: Smooth haze fade-ins/outs matching lighting transitions (2–4 bar changes)

### Haze + Light Interaction
- Warm amber/gold light looks "honeyed" in haze — this is the target aesthetic
- Cool blue light in heavy haze can feel harsh — use sparingly
- Haze amplifies mover movement visually — use as a tool to extend perceived motion
- Mid-frequency gobos and prisms are more visible in haze, but keep them minimal in house

## Venue Scale Notes

House lighting philosophy adapts to venue size, but the warm foundation remains constant.

### Small Clubs (500–1000 capacity)
- **Par usage**: Essential — high intensity wash is the primary visual
- **Mover usage**: 1–2 movers accent, not lead
- **Haze**: Light to medium (prevent overcrowding the space)
- **Color intensity**: Warmer, less contrast (warm white, amber, gold dominate)
- **Effect density**: Minimal — effects are visible at close range, less is more

### Medium Clubs (1000–3000 capacity)
- **Par usage**: Full par setup, par washes dominate
- **Mover usage**: 3–4 movers, slow sweeping, gentle positioning
- **Haze**: Medium (build beam definition for sightlines across the room)
- **Color intensity**: Mix of warm foundation with cool accents for contrast
- **Effect density**: Moderate — gobos/prisms appear occasionally

### Large Venues / Arenas (3000+ capacity)
- **Par usage**: Unified par washes create the "dancefloor glow" visible from all sections
- **Mover usage**: 4+ movers, wider sweeps, more dramatic positioning
- **Haze**: Medium to heavy (makes large beam patterns visible from the back)
- **Color intensity**: More contrast and peak color saturation for visibility
- **Effect density**: Increased — gobos/prisms used more frequently in builds/peaks

### The Constant
Regardless of venue size, **warm amber/gold is always the foundation.** Scale affects intensity
and complexity, not the warm aesthetic.

## Subgenre Variations

### Deep House
- **Palette**: Darker, more minimal (deep blues, teals, purples) with warm gold accents
- **Pars**: Warm undertone even in cool moments (never pure white or cold blue)
- **Movement**: Less movement, more atmosphere; movers often static or very slow
- **Par role**: Pars doing most of the work (80%+ visual)
- **Energy**: Hypnotic, minimal, introspective
- **References**: DC-10 Ibiza aesthetic — industrial space, monochromatic warmth

### Tech House
- **Palette**: Cooler palette (cyans, blues, whites) with gold as warmth anchor
- **Pars**: Slightly cooler than melodic house, but never lose warmth
- **Movement**: More rhythmic movement tied to the groove, tighter sequences
- **Timing**: Snappier transitions (still smooth, but tighter than progressive)
- **Strobe**: Slightly more acceptable (2–3 short bursts per peak, not sustained)
- **Energy**: Mechanical, hypnotic, rhythmic
- **Effect density**: More gobo rotation and prism use than deep house

### Progressive House
- **Palette**: Widest emotional range — warm opening, cool breakdown, warm climax
- **Pars**: Dominant par washes that evolve throughout track
- **Movement**: Biggest mover sweeps of any house subgenre (4–8 bar movements)
- **Builds**: Longest builds and most dramatic peaks
- **Transitions**: Smoothest crossfades (all 4–8 bar fades minimum)
- **Energy**: Epic, emotional, cinematic
- **Timing**: Slowest timing presets (8-bar fades common)

### Melodic House
- **Palette**: Warmest palette (ambers, pinks, magentas) with intimate atmosphere
- **Pars**: Warm washes that mirror melodic contour
- **Movement**: Movers follow melody (fast passages = more movement, slow passages = static)
- **Breaks**: Most intimate breakdowns (single color, low intensity)
- **Colors**: Magenta and pink used more freely at peaks
- **Energy**: Warm, emotional, human-focused
- **Philosophy**: Lighting follows the melody, not the kick

## Notable References

### Real-World House Lighting Examples

**DC-10 Ibiza (Deep House)**
- Setting: Industrial warehouse, raw concrete
- Aesthetic: Monochromatic warm amber/red wash (single color focus)
- Philosophy: Minimal effects, let the structure and warmth speak
- Lighting profile: High-intensity pars, no (or very minimal) mover movement
- Lesson: Warmth alone can sustain an entire night of lighting

**Hi Ibiza (Melodic / Progressive House)**
- Setting: Modern club with polished production
- Aesthetic: Kinetic mirror effects, Robe Pointe movers, warm washes
- Philosophy: Elegant movement paired with disco-inspired color rotation
- Lighting profile: Balanced par/mover mix, slow sweeps, mirror ball tradition
- Lesson: Movement enhances without overwhelming; honor disco heritage

**The Warehouse (Chicago House Origins)**
- Setting: Minimal industrial space, bassline-focused
- Aesthetic: Simple colored lights, strobe on kick, warm undertone
- Philosophy: Support the groove, don't distract from it
- Lighting profile: Par-heavy, sparse effects, rhythm-focused strobes
- Lesson: Simplicity is strength; let the music carry the show

### Design Principles from the Culture

1. **"Lighting DJ" mindset**: An LD should read the crowd like a DJ reads dancers. Smooth transitions,
   responsive energy, always building. Never shock; always flow.

2. **The four-on-the-floor pulse**: Every lighting element should acknowledge the kick in some way.
   Even the most static look has a subtle pulse or breathing quality that matches 4/4 timing.

3. **Sidechain breathing**: Modern house production uses sidechain compression — the kick ducks
   everything else. Lighting should mimic this with subtle breathing effects on pars (subtle
   brightness pulses) and smooth, responsive mover motion.

4. **Disco > Rave**: House inherited disco's philosophy (audience as part of the light show, warm
   glow) not rave's (shock effects, rapid changes). Lighting should reflect this lineage.

5. **The "dancefloor glow"**: The best house lighting makes the dancefloor feel alive with warmth.
   This is the ultimate goal — not to impress with effects, but to create an environment where
   people want to dance.

## Key Philosophy

House lighting is about **warmth, flow, groove, and cultural respect**. Everything should feel
smooth and interconnected — never jarring. The groove comes first; lighting should enhance the
rhythmic feeling, not overpower it.

The genre's lineage runs through disco: a "dancefloor glow" where the audience becomes part of
the light show, not a spectator. The LD is a "lighting DJ," reading the crowd's energy and
building smoothly through crossfades, subtle breathing effects, and warm palettes. The best
house lighting makes you feel the music more deeply through warmth and flow, not shock effects.

**Remember**: Warm colors (Disco Gold, Amber, Warm White) are non-negotiable. Pars are the
foundation, not an afterthought. Every transition should honor the four-on-the-floor pulse.
Simplicity is strength.
