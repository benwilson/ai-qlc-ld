# Genre: House

## Overview

House music encompasses a wide range from deep and minimal to progressive and tech. The
unifying thread is a steady four-on-the-floor kick at 120–130 BPM with groove-based energy
that builds gradually rather than dropping explosively. Lighting should feel warm, flowing,
and rhythmic — enhancing the groove rather than overwhelming it.

## BPM Range

- Typical: 120–130 BPM
- Common: 124–128 BPM
- Time signature: 4/4 (four-on-the-floor kick)

## Energy Profile

House has a more gradual energy curve than bass music genres. Transitions between sections
are smoother, and the "drop" is often more of an arrival than an explosion.

| Section | Energy | Duration | Lighting Character |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------------|
| Intro | Low | 8–16 bars | Warm wash, minimal movement, establish groove |
| Groove Build | Low–Medium | 8–16 bars | Gentle evolution, add layers one at a time |
| Melodic Rise | Medium | 8–16 bars | Colors deepen, movers widen, add texture |
| Peak/Drop | High | 16–32 bars | Full palette, sweeping movers, smooth and joyful |
| Emotional Break | Medium | 8–16 bars | Strip back, intimate feel, single color family |
| Final Build | Medium–High | 8–16 bars | Layer everything back, build to climax |
| Outro | Low | 8–16 bars | Return to opening feel, gentle fade |

## Color Palette

### Primary Colors
- **Warm White** (255, 180, 100) — inviting, club atmosphere, the foundation
- **Deep Blue** (0, 0, 255) / BSW Blue — late night, deep house moods
- **Magenta** (255, 0, 255) / BSW Magenta — vibrant, disco heritage, peak moments

### Accent Colors
- **Amber** (255, 128, 0) / Sharpy Amber — warmth, golden hour, soul
- **Teal** (0, 255, 128) / BSW Teal / Sharpy Teal — fresh, deep, sophisticated
- **Pink** (255, 64, 128) / BSW Pink / Sharpy Pink — playful, melodic moments
- **Cyan** (0, 255, 255) — clean, modern, tech house

### Two-Color Pairs
| Combo Name | Color A | Color B | Best For |
|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| Sunset | Amber | Magenta | Warm peaks, melodic house |
| Deep Ocean | Deep Blue | Teal | Deep house grooves, late night |
| Club Classic | Magenta | White | Disco-inspired drops |
| Golden Hour | Warm White | Amber | Opening, intimate moments |
| Cool Dawn | Cyan | Blue | Tech house, minimal |
| Neon Rose | Pink | Magenta | Melodic peaks, emotional |
| Night Sky | Deep Blue | Purple | Atmospheric breakdowns |

### Avoid
- Harsh red as a primary (too aggressive for house energy)
- Rapid color snaps (house is about flow, not shock)
- Monochrome white/red/green (too "rock concert" or "industrial")

## Movement Conventions

### Peak Movement
- Smooth 2–4 bar sweeps, never jarring snaps
- Figure-8 or wide arc patterns on movers
- Gentle spread from center to wide positions
- Movers should feel like they're dancing, not fighting

### Groove Sections
- Slow, continuous drift (8+ bar movements)
- Subtle left-right oscillation matching the groove
- Movers staying relatively close to center
- Movement driven by the bassline, not the kick

### Breakdown Movement
- Converge movers to center or DJ booth
- Slow, intimate — like a spotlight on the moment
- Optional: ceiling hit for ethereal break with haze

### Build Movement
- Gradual spread from center to wide (over 8–16 bars)
- Smooth, never accelerating dramatically
- Color evolution rather than position changes

| Preset | FadeIn (ms) | Hold (ms) | Use |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-----|
| House Smooth 2bar | 4000 | 0 | Smooth color transitions (2 bars at 128) |
| House Smooth 4bar | 8000 | 0 | Extended smooth sweeps |
| House Smooth 8bar | 16000 | 0 | Slow atmospheric fades |
| House Beat | 0 | 500 | Beat-synced accents (1 beat at 128) |
| House 2Beat | 0 | 1000 | 2-beat hold for subtle chase |
| House Bar | 500 | 1500 | Soft snap per bar |
| House Breathe | 4000 | 4000 | Gentle 4-bar pulse (2 bar fade + 2 bar hold) |
| House Strobe | 0 | 125 | 1/4 beat strobe (use sparingly) |

## Strobe Conventions

- **Sparingly**: House is not a strobe-heavy genre — reserve for the biggest peak moments
- **Short bursts**: 2–4 beats maximum, then back to smooth
- **Color strobe**: Magenta or cyan strobe feels more "house" than white
- **Par flash**: Use par strobe (ch4 or ch2) for subtle rhythmic flash rather than full rig strobe
- **Never**: Sustained strobe through an entire section

## Laser Conventions

- **Peaks only**: Lasers on during the biggest peak moments, 4–8 bars maximum
- **Subtle**: Single laser color matching the palette, not all-three blast
- **Accent**: Brief laser reveal at the top of a build for a "wow" moment
- **No strobe**: Laser strobe feels too aggressive for house — steady on or off

## Gobo & Prism Conventions

- **Peaks**: Open gobo with optional single prism for texture
- **Grooves**: Open gobo, clean beams, focus on color and position
- **Breakdowns**: Open gobo, no prism — clean and minimal
- **Optional**: Slow gobo rotation during extended breakdown for subtle texture

## Par Behavior

- **Peaks**: `par_wash()` in warm, unified color for maximum cohesion
- **Grooves**: `par_gradient()` for depth and sophistication
- **Breakdowns**: `par_wash()` at medium intensity, warm tones
- **Builds**: `par_gradient()` evolving through color families

## Subgenre Variations

### Deep House
- Darker, more minimal palette (blues, teals, purples)
- Less movement, more atmosphere
- Movers often static or very slow
- Pars doing most of the work

### Tech House
- Cooler palette (cyans, blues, whites)
- More rhythmic movement tied to the groove
- Slightly more strobe acceptable
- Tighter, more controlled looks

### Progressive House
- Wider emotional range — warm to cool across a track
- Bigger builds and more dramatic peaks
- More mover movement in peaks
- Longest sweeps of any house subgenre

### Melodic House
- Warmest palette (ambers, pinks, magentas)
- Emotional lighting that follows the melody
- Smooth crossfades matching melodic phrases
- Most intimate breakdowns

## Key Philosophy

House lighting is about **warmth, flow, and groove**. Everything should feel smooth and
interconnected — never jarring. The groove comes first; lighting should enhance the rhythmic
feeling, not overpower it. Use smooth crossfades (2–4 bars), warm palettes, and restrained
effects. The best house lighting makes you feel the music more deeply, not just see it.
