# Liquid Drum & Bass

## Overview

Liquid drum and bass is the emotional, vocal-driven side of the genre. Where neurofunk is aggressive and chaotic, liquid is flowing and beautiful. The fast 170+ BPM tempo carries smooth, warm atmospheres, soulful breakdowns, and joyful rather than predatory drops. Lighting should emphasize warmth, organic movement, and smooth color evolution — creating an intimate and uplifting space despite the speed underneath.

Core mood: "Beautiful grooves rolling forward, warm enveloping light, vocal intimacy, floating energy."

## BPM Range

**Typical**: 170–178 BPM
**Standard**: 174 BPM
**Slower liquid**: 160–168 BPM (jazzy, very mellow)
**Faster liquid**: 180–185 BPM (still smooth, slightly more urgent)

### Two-Color Pairs (Gradient / Chase Patterns)

1. **Amber → Ocean Blue** (warm to cool, liquid signature) — for par gradients and mover sweeps
2. **Soft White → Teal** (ethereal → mysterious) — intro/outro, ambient sections
3. **Warm Amber → Rose Pink** (intimate warmth) — vocal spotlights, breakdowns
4. **Ocean Blue → Purple** (cool → mysterious) — second drop, builds
5. **Teal → Soft White** (mystery → clarity) — transition sections
6. **Rose Pink → Amber** (pink warmth) — accent texture, emotional peaks

### Colors to Avoid

- **Hard reds** (cold, aggressive) — liquid is not violent. Avoid pure red; use rose pink or amber instead.
- **Harsh greens** (sickly, unfamiliar in liquid) — green is rare; only use in teal/cyan form for depth.
- **Bright yellows** (clownish, distracting) — stick to warm amber, not neon yellow.
- **Pure white + hard strobes** (too jarring) — white is atmospheric wash only; strobes are rare and slow.

## Movement Conventions

### Intro (Bars 1–16, Energy 2–3)

- **Movers**: Idle or single slow figure-8 at floor center. Pan very slow (4–8 bar cycle). Tilt gently oscillating.
- **Pars**: Soft wash, single color (soft white or low-intensity amber).
- **Goal**: Create calm, enveloping atmosphere. Audience should feel safe.

### Vocal Build (Bars 17–32, Energy 4–6)

- **Movers**: Introduce slow 4–8 bar linear sweeps (e.g., stage left → center, center → stage right). Keep figure-8 slow and deliberate.
- **Timing**: Crossfade time ≥ 4 bars between positions. No snapping.
- **Pars**: Gradually add fixtures (1–2 pars per 8 bars). Blend warm amber + soft white.
- **Goal**: Build anticipation smoothly. Every new movement or color should feel like a natural unfolding, not a surprise.

### Drop / Main Section (Bars 33–64, Energy 7–8)

- **Movers**: Full figure-8 at medium speed (2–4 bar cycles), or converging sweeps (all movers move toward center, then diverge in 4–6 bar arcs).
- **Timing**: 2–4 bar smooth crossfades between positions. Movement is flowing but confident.
- **Pars**: Full wash (all 6 pars lit). Use gradient: Amber (P1/P3) → Blue (P2/P4) → Teal (missyees). Maintains warm foundation while adding color texture.
- **Strobes**: Minimal or none. If strobe is used, very slow pulse (1–2 per bar at 174 BPM, soft and gentle).
- **Lasers**: None.
- **Goal**: Fill the space with flowing light and movement. The energy is joyful and full, not aggressive.

### Rolling Section (Bars 65–96, Energy 7–8)

- **Movers**: Gentle, steady oscillation. Slow figure-8 or slow converge/diverge cycles (4–8 bar smooth). Movement should feel like "breathing."
- **Pars**: Warm gradient dominates. Example: Amber on stage left/right (P1, P3), soft white center (P2, P4), teal wash from missyees. Gradient slowly shifts color (8+ bar smooth transitions).
- **Timing**: All transitions ≥ 4 bars. This section is the heart of liquid — let it breathe.
- **Strobes**: None.
- **Lasers**: None.
- **Goal**: Sustain the groove. Listener should feel wrapped in warm, gentle light that slowly evolves. No surprises.

### Breakdown (Bars 97–112, Energy 4–5)

- **Movers**: Converge toward center (8+ bar smooth). Final position: dead center, minimal movement. Tilt can point downward slightly for intimacy.
- **Pars**: Single color wash (warm amber or soft white). Intensity steady or slowly fading. Minimal fixtures (maybe 2–3 pars instead of all 6) for spotlight effect.
- **Strobes**: None.
- **Lasers**: None.
- **Goal**: Isolate and spotlight vocals or melody. Light becomes intimate and minimal. Audience focus narrows.

### Second Drop / Climax (Bars 113–136, Energy 8–9)

- **Movers**: Return to flowing figure-8 or wider converging sweeps. Speed can increase slightly (2–4 bar cycles, or faster if final climax). Tilt oscillation widens.
- **Pars**: Full palette. Amber + ocean blue + teal all active. Can use par chase (one par highlighting per 1–2 bars) or gradient (slower, smoother).
- **Strobes**: Optional light pulse (sparse, 1–2 per bar). Keep soft — liquid aesthetics. Not chaotic.
- **Lasers**: Optional sparse laser bursts (1–2 per 4 bars) for accent only. Poetic, not aggressive.
- **Goal**: Final joyful crest. More energy than main drop, but still flowing and warm.

### Outro (Bars 137–152, Energy 2–4)

- **Movers**: Slow fade to idle. Return to gentle figure-8 or simple pan drift.
- **Pars**: Fade to soft white wash, then dim. Remove color layering.
- **Strobes**: None.
- **Lasers**: None.
- **Goal**: Graceful return to calm. End on intimate, ambient tone.

## Timing Presets

**Calculations at 174 BPM** (quarter note = 344 ms, eighth note = 172 ms)

| Duration | Bars | Milliseconds | Use Case |
|----------|------|--------------|----------|
| 1 beat | 0.25 | 86 ms | (rare in liquid) |
| 1 bar | 4 | 344 × 4 = 1,376 ms | Snap transitions (rare) |
| 2 bars | 8 | 2,752 ms | Fast movement cycle, pulse timing |
| 4 bars | 16 | 5,504 ms | Standard smooth crossfade, mover sweep start/end |
| 6 bars | 24 | 8,256 ms | Extended smooth buildup crossfade |
| 8 bars | 32 | 11,008 ms | Liquid signature smooth crossfade, full par blend |
| 12 bars | 48 | 16,512 ms | Slow color evolution, section-to-section fade |
| 16 bars | 64 | 22,016 ms | Full intro/breakdown arc, slowest atmospheric shift |

**General rules**:
- Crossfade time ≥ 4 bars (5.5 sec minimum). Liquid is never abrupt.
- Strobe pulses (if used): 1–2 per bar, held for 50–100 ms each (pulse only, not persistent).
- Par gradient shifts: 8+ bars for smooth color evolution.
- Mover figure-8 cycle: 4–8 bars round trip (2–4 bars per axis).

## Strobe Conventions

**Philosophy**: Strobes are rare in liquid. The genre prioritizes warmth and flowing light over rhythmic flashing.

- **Drop (main)**: Optional slow pulse only (1–2 per bar). Soft intensity (not full brightness). Creates gentle texture without aggression.
- **Second drop**: Optional sparse pulse (1–2 per 4 bars) for accent only.
- **All other sections**: None.

**Fixture strobe channels**:
- **Sharpy** (Ch6): Use `SHARPY_STROBE_SLOW` (value ~20–40) for gentle pulse. Keep open/closed range high (use 252 = open as baseline, never snap to 0).
- **BSW** (Ch16): Use `BSW_SHUT_STROBE_SLOW` (values 18–40) for gentle pulse.
- **Profile** (Ch1): Use slow strobe values (5–20 for slow pulse).
- **Duration**: 50–100 ms on, 250+ ms off.

**Avoid**: Harsh strobes, fast strobes, combined strobe + laser, strobe on all fixtures simultaneously.

## Laser Conventions

**Philosophy**: Lasers are optional and poetic in liquid. They accent moments, not dominate.

- **Drops**: None (distracting from warmth).
- **Second drop / climax only**: Optional sparse bursts (1–2 per 4 bars, 100–200 ms each) for emotional accent.
- **Breakdown**: None (kills intimacy).
- **Intro/outro**: None.

**Fixture laser channels** (NI3K only):
- **Red laser** (Ch14): `LASER_ON` (250) or `LASER_STROBE_SLOW` (160–190).
- **Green laser** (Ch15): `LASER_ON` (250) or `LASER_STROBE_SLOW` (160–190).
- **Blue laser** (Ch16): `LASER_ON` (250) or `LASER_STROBE_SLOW` (160–190).
- Use single-color laser (e.g., green only) for poetic effect. Three-color laser together can read as chaotic.
- **Duration**: 100–200 ms on per burst, 400+ ms gap between bursts.

## Gobo & Prism Conventions

**Philosophy**: Gobos and prisms are minimal in liquid. The genre is about color and movement, not texture overlay.

- **Gobos**: Off in most sections. Optional soft-edge gobo (G1 or G2 on BSW) during breaks for subtle detail, but keep shutter open enough that gobo is readable (no hard edge).
- **Prisms**: Off in most sections. Optional slow prism rotation (1 rev per 8–12 bars) on Sharpy or BSW during rolling sections for delicate texture, but not dominant.

**Fixture gobo channels**:
- **BSW** (Ch9–10): Keep at 0 (open) by default. Optional: `BSW_G1_OPEN` to `BSW_G1_3` for soft texture.
- **Sharpy** (Ch10): Keep at 0 (open) by default. Gobos are dark/aggressive on Sharpy (not liquid aesthetic).

**Fixture prism channels**:
- **Sharpy** (Ch11–12): `0` = off. Keep off unless rolling section calls for delicate texture.
- **BSW** (Ch13–14): `0` = off. Keep off by default.

## Par Behavior

**Philosophy**: Pars are the foundation of liquid's warm atmosphere. They create color washes and gradients that evoke intimacy and flow.

### Par Layouts (6 pars + 2 missyees)

**Intro / ambient**:
- Single wash: Soft white or low amber across all pars.
- Intensity: 50–60% (not full brightness, atmospheric).

**Vocal build**:
- Gradient build: Start with amber (P1, P3) at 30%, add soft white (P2, P4) at 40%, missyees stay warm.
- Smooth fade-in over 8+ bars.

**Drop / main**:
- Full gradient: Amber (P1, P3, left/right wings) → Soft white (P2, P4, center) → Teal (missyees, stage front).
- All at 80–100% intensity. Pars feel "open" and enveloping.
- Optional: Slow par color evolution over 8 bars (amber → blue → amber cycle for gradient drift).

**Rolling section**:
- Warm gradient dominates: Amber stage left/right, soft white center, teal front. Intensity steady 75–100%.
- Optional: Slow color shift every 8 bars (amber gradually transitions toward pink, then back). Creates gentle "breathing" in the palette.

**Breakdown**:
- Single wash: Warm amber or soft white. Intensity 30–50% (spotlight feel, minimal coverage).
- Or: All pars off except P2 + P4 (center pars) on warm amber for true spotlight.

**Second drop**:
- Full palette, higher energy: Amber + blue + teal all bright. Intensity 90–100%.
- Optional par chase: One par cycles through colors per 1–2 bars (energetic but not chaotic).

**Outro**:
- Fade to soft white, dim over 8+ bars. Intensity slowly drops to 10–20% then off.

## Fixture Layering

**Priority order** (build your scenes in this order for cohesive depth):

1. **Pars** (foundation wash, color ground)
2. **Movers** (movement, tilt pointing, frost for atmosphere)
3. **Missyees** (accent color, texture fill)
4. **NI3K** (halo glow, laser accents, if used)

**Mover focus hierarchy**:
- Main movers (Sharpy, BSW, Profile): Follow movement conventions (sweeps, converge, figure-8).
- Tilt/frost: Keep tilt centered unless pointing to specific stage areas. Frost on 30–50% for soft diffusion.
- During drops: Frost off (hard beam), movers bright and visible.
- During breakdowns: Frost on (soft beam), movers dim or idle.

**Missyee role**:
- Color accent, not solo feature.
- Generally follow par color but offset (if pars are warm, missyees are cool and vice versa).
- During intro/outro: Off or very dim.
- During drops: Bright teal or blue for depth.

**NI3K role** (if available):
- Halo as accent color only (use `H_OFF` most of the time, activate for emotional peaks).
- Lasers: Optional poetic accents in second drop (1–2 bursts per 4 bars).
- Tilt: Can mirror mover figure-8 or rotate slowly (low speed) for chaotic texture (rare, use sparingly).

## Haze & Atmosphere

**Use haze to enhance liquid's warm, enveloping aesthetic**:

- **Light haze** (low density) throughout show for beam visibility and atmosphere.
- **Thicker haze during breakdowns** to create spotlight effect around warm-colored movers.
- **Haze density increases** during builds and second drop (supports energetic moment).
- **Haze clears** during outro (visual reset toward calm).

**Effect on lighting**:
- Haze softens color transitions and makes warm colors (amber, orange) feel more diffused and intimate.
- Haze also makes blue and teal feel deeper and cooler (good contrast to warm amber base).
- Strobes + haze = visible texture (good for sparse laser moments). Strobes + no haze = distracting glitter (avoid).

## Venue Scale Notes

### Small Venue (Home Studio, 10m × 8m)

- Use all fixtures to fill the room (small space = concentrated energy).
- Par gradients feel more intimate; rely heavily on color rather than large movement arcs.
- Keep mover sweeps tight (converge to near-center instead of far walls).
- Frost on movers = softer beam fills more of the room (good for small spaces).
- Haze adds perceived depth (makes small room feel bigger).

### Medium Venue (150–300 capacity, 20m × 15m)

- Balanced use of movers + pars. Movement becomes more expressive (wider arcs, longer sweeps).
- Par gradients are visible and impactful from all angles.
- Multiple movers can move in opposition (one stage left while another moves stage right) for dynamic fill.
- Haze thickness moderate (too much = unclear movement; too little = harsh beams).

### Large Venue (500+ capacity, 40m+ stage)

- Movers dominate with wide, bold sweeps (small movement becomes invisible from back of room).
- Pars provide area washes; movers provide detail and focus.
- Figure-8 and converge/diverge patterns are the primary vocabulary (simple, reads from far back).
- Haze critical for beam visibility. Expect visible laser paths and defined par washes.
- Color saturation matters more than subtlety (pale pastels read as grey from distance).

## Key Philosophy

**Liquid drum and bass lighting is the opposite of neurofunk's chaos.**

- **Warmth over coldness**: Every scene should feel enveloping and safe, not alienating.
- **Flow over snap**: Crossfades are ≥ 4 bars. Movement is smooth. No abrupt color jumps.
- **Atmosphere over aggression**: Strobes are rare, lasers are poetic accents, haze is thick, frost is on. The speed of the beat is hidden under warm light.
- **Emotional peaks, not technical peaks**: Lighting serves the vocal, the melody, the emotional arc — not the kick drum.
- **Gradients > single colors**: The eye should travel across warm amber → cool blue → teal in flowing transitions, not hard color blocks.
- **Figure-8 and converge > chaotic rotation**: Movement is beautiful and organic, like breathing or floating, not aggressive spinning.
- **Pars are the base**: Movers add detail, but pars create the fundamental atmosphere. Liquid feels most at home in a warm, gradient-washed room.

When building a liquid show, ask:
- *Does the audience feel safe and enveloped?*
- *Can they follow the vocal and melody through the light?*
- *Is every transition smooth enough that they don't jolt?*
- *Does the warm amber palette feel like home?*

If yes to all four, the lighting is liquid.
