# Genre: Trance

## Overview

Trance is built on euphoric melodies, long emotional builds, and cathartic releases. It's the
most "emotional journey" genre of EDM — a single track can take the listener from delicate
beauty through intense tension to overwhelming release. Lighting should follow this emotional
arc with sweeping movements, rich color evolution, and dramatic reveals. The breakdown is the
most sacred moment (not the drop) — treat it with intimacy and patience. The goal is to make
the audience FEEL something transcendent.

## BPM Range

- Typical: 130–150 BPM
- Modern standard: 140 BPM
- Common: 138–142 BPM
- Time signature: 4/4

## Energy Profile

Trance has the longest build-release cycles of any EDM genre. Breakdowns are the emotional
peak (not the drops), and the release after a breakdown is cathartic rather than aggressive.

| Section | Energy | Duration | Lighting Character |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------------|
| Intro | Low | 16–32 bars | Ethereal wash, stars/gobo texture, gentle |
| Build 1 | Low–Medium | 16–32 bars | Slow color evolution, movers gradually widening |
| First Release | High | 16–32 bars | Full rig, sweeping movements, joyful colors |
| Extended Breakdown | Low–Medium | 16–32 bars | THE emotional peak — single color, intimate, beautiful |
| Main Build | Medium–High | 8–16 bars | Rising intensity, all elements layering in |
| Main Release | Peak | 16–32 bars | Full euphoria — maximum color, movement, lasers |
| Outro | Low | 8–16 bars | Gentle fade, return to ethereal |

## Color Palette

### Primary Colors
- **Blue** (0, 0, 255) / BSW_BLUE / Sharpy Blue (value 30) — THE signature trance color, sky and ocean, contemplative
- **White** (255, 255, 255) / BSW_WHITE — euphoria, release, transcendence, wall of light
- **Cyan** (0, 255, 255) / Sharpy Teal (value 70) — clean, celestial, uplifting, clean beams
- **Celestial White** (200, 200, 255) — slightly blue-white wash for releases, softer than pure white

### Accent Colors
- **Purple** (128, 0, 255) / Sharpy Purple (value 50) — emotional depth, mystical, build phases
- **Magenta** (255, 0, 255) / BSW_MAG — passionate, uplifting moments, warm releases
- **Green** (0, 255, 0) / BSW_GREEN / Sharpy Green (value 40) — nature, outdoors, festival trance only
- **Warm Pink** (255, 100, 150) / Sharpy Pink (value 60) — emotional breakdowns, vulnerable moments

### Two-Color Pairs
| Combo Name | Color A | Color B | Best For |
|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| Sky | Blue | Cyan | Classic trance, euphoric release |
| Ethereal | Blue | White | Clean, uplifting peaks |
| Mystic | Purple | Blue | Deep breakdowns, emotional builds |
| Aurora | Cyan | Green | Festival trance, outdoors (not club) |
| Passion | Magenta | Warm Pink | Emotional breakdowns, vulnerable moments |
| Celestial | Celestial White | Cyan | Maximum euphoria moments, releases |
| Sunset | Magenta | Amber | Warm emotional peaks |

### Color Evolution Journey
The classic trance arc through a track:
1. **Intro**: Deep blue/cyan (cool, contemplative)
2. **Build 1**: Blue → Purple → Magenta (warming, emotional tension building)
3. **First Release**: Bright cyan/white (uplifting, but not peak)
4. **Breakdown**: Single pure color — blue, cyan, or warm pink (intimate, vulnerable)
5. **Main Build**: Purple → Magenta → White (rising warmth and intensity)
6. **Main Release**: Explosion into white/magenta/bright cyan (transcendent euphoria)

### Avoid
- Dark, aggressive colors as primaries (red, dark purple — wrong mood)
- Black/darkness as a primary tool (trance is about light, not shadow)
- Harsh, angular color changes (trance is flowing)
- Green in club settings (festival only)
- Hot pink — use warm pink instead

## Movement Conventions

### Release/Peak Movement
- Wide, sweeping arcs — the biggest, most dramatic mover movements
- All movers synchronized in large-scale sweeps (L→R over 4–8 bars)
- Figure-8 and circular patterns at medium speed
- Movements should feel "soaring" — matching the melodic euphoria
- NI3K slow rotation with full RGBW for ambient texture
- Beam convergence from edges to center creates "cathedral" effect

### Breakdown Movement
- Movers converge slowly to center or DJ booth position
- Extremely slow, deliberate movement (16+ bar sweeps, the signature "Trance Sweep 16bar" preset)
- The movement should feel intimate, like the room is shrinking
- Ceiling hit with single color for "starfield" effect in haze
- Parallel vertical beams create "beams from heaven" look
- All movers should hold the converged position for several bars (patience is key)

### Build Movement
- Movers gradually spreading from center to wide (over 8–16 bars)
- Speed increasing from very slow to moderate
- Color shifting from cool to warm (blue → purple → magenta) over the build
- Add sweep width as build progresses
- Layer in new elements every 4–8 bars to maintain tension

### Intro/Outro
- Minimal movement, movers at center or ceiling
- Gentle drift, barely perceptible (use sub-4-bar fade times)
- Focus on color atmosphere, not position

## Timing Presets

At 140 BPM (modern trance standard):
- 1 beat = 428.6 ms
- 1 bar (4 beats) = 1714.3 ms
- 4 bars = 6857 ms
- 8 bars = 13714 ms
- 16 bars = 27429 ms
- 32 bars = 54857 ms

## Strobe Conventions

- **Rare**: Trance uses significantly less strobe than most EDM genres
- **Release moments only**: Brief strobe burst (2–4 beats) at the top of a main release
- **White or cyan**: Color strobes if needed, matching the palette
- **Par strobe preferred**: Pars flashing rhythmically feels more "trance" than moving head strobe
- **Never during breakdowns**: Breakdowns are sacred — no strobe, no flash, no aggression
- **Festival trance exception**: Festival mainstage shows may use more strobe during the main release (spectacle requirement)

## Laser Conventions

- **Release sections**: Lasers during the main release for maximum euphoria — this is THE trance moment
- **All RGB spread**: Trance is one of the few genres where full RGB laser spread works well (creates "laser cathedral" effect)
- **Sweeping patterns**: NI3K pan sweep with RGB lasers creates dramatic room-filling effects, full horizontal and vertical coverage
- **Not for breakdowns**: Lasers off during emotional sections — the breakdown is laser-free
- **Laser dominance**: Trance is the most laser-heavy EDM genre — lasers are a PRIMARY fixture type, not an accent
- **Festival trance**: Massive laser arrays, often filling the entire room with parallel/crossing beams
- **Club trance**: More restrained but still visible — focus on clean geometric patterns (parallel verticals, horizontal sweeps)
- **Ceiling-to-audience**: Beams should extend from stage into the audience space, making the whole room the light show

## Gobo & Prism Conventions

- **Breakdowns**: Soft gobo for texture (not harsh patterns), slow or no rotation — organic/flowing shapes preferred
- **Releases**: Open gobo for clean beams, optional prism for multi-beam spread to fill more space
- **Builds**: Add gobo texture partway through (bar 8-12 of the build), then clear to open at release
- **Preferred gobos**: Organic/flowing patterns (rings, waves, stars) rather than sharp geometric ones (triangles, grids)
- **Prism during sweeps**: Running prism rotation during wide 8+ bar sweeps multiplies beam coverage without adding fixtures

## Par Behavior

- **Releases**: `par_wash()` in bright, unified color — maximum euphoria, "wall of light" effect
- **Grooves**: `par_gradient()` flowing through cool color families (blue → cyan → purple)
- **Breakdowns**: `par_wash()` single intimate color at medium-low intensity, supporting the converged movers
- **Builds**: `par_gradient()` evolving from cool to warm over the build (blue → purple → magenta)
- **Warm moments**: Shift pars toward amber/magenta for emotional release sections

## Fixture Layering

Trance uses a distinctive fixture hierarchy that mirrors the emotional journey:

### Breakdown Section (Emotional Peak)
1. **Foundation**: Single color wash from pars (blue or cyan) at medium intensity
2. **Accent**: Converged movers at center/ceiling pointing downward, same color as pars
3. **Texture**: Soft gobo on movers, slow or stopped rotation
4. **Atmosphere**: NI3K halo (if present) at low intensity, either off or matching the color
5. **Lasers**: OFF — breakdowns are laser-free for intimacy

### Build Section (Rising Tension)
1. **Foundation**: Pars shift from cool to warm (blue → purple → magenta)
2. **Layer 1 (bars 1-4)**: Movers begin spreading from center, same color as pars
3. **Layer 2 (bars 5-8)**: Add gobo texture to movers, introduce second color (purple)
4. **Layer 3 (bars 9-12)**: Widen mover sweeps to full room, color continues shifting
5. **Layer 4 (bars 13-16)**: Add prism rotation, introduce magenta/white accents
6. **Lasers**: OFF during build (save for release)

### Main Release (Euphoria Peak)
1. **Foundation**: All pars at full brightness, unified white or bright cyan
2. **Primary**: Wide synchronized mover sweeps with full RGB lighting, executing large figure-8s or horizontal sweeps
3. **Accent**: NI3K high-rotation movement with full RGBW, creating ambient texture throughout the room
4. **Prism + Gobo**: Open gobo with prism rotation for multi-beam fill
5. **Lasers**: FULL RGB laser spread, sweeping patterns or parallel beams filling the entire room from stage to audience
6. **Intensity**: Every fixture at maximum or near-maximum intensity — "wall of light"
7. **Movement**: Everything synchronized in large-scale patterns, creating unified visual energy

### Intro/Outro (Contemplative)
1. **Foundation**: Single color wash from pars (deep blue or cyan) at low-medium intensity
2. **Movers**: Minimal movement, positioned at center or ceiling, very slow transitions
3. **Gobos**: Soft star or organic pattern, no rotation
4. **Accents**: NI3K at low intensity with slow halo color drift
5. **Lasers**: OFF

## Haze & Atmosphere

Trance requires careful haze management to achieve the "cathedral" and "beams from heaven" effects that are central to the genre:

### Haze Intensity
- **Medium-heavy baseline**: Enough haze for beams to be visible during releases and to create atmosphere
- **Breakdown focus**: Increase haze slightly during emotional breakdowns to make converged movers create a "starfield" effect
- **Release maximum**: Maintain consistent haze level through the release — too much and the brightness is lost, too little and the "ceiling fill" effect disappears
- **Intro/outro**: Lighter haze, matching the introspection
- **Avoid**: Oppressive haze that makes the room feel heavy or dark (wrong for trance's euphoric character)

### Haze + Beam Interaction
- **Vertical beams (breakdown)**: Haze makes converged movers pointing downward create a "shower of light" effect
- **Horizontal sweeps (release)**: Haze makes wide L-R and circular sweeps visible across the entire room width
- **Laser + haze**: Trance's defining "laser cathedral" look requires haze to make laser beams distinct (without haze, lasers look like moving dots)
- **Par wash + haze**: Pars in haze create a gentle glow that softens the room ambiance

### Practical Guidelines
- **Festival trance**: Heavier haze (closer to medium-heavy), supports massive laser arrays and large venue scale
- **Club trance**: Medium haze, supports intimate feeling while maintaining beam visibility
- **During breakdowns**: Haze can drift higher for 8-16 bars to enhance the vulnerable moment
- **Haze consistency**: Maintain relatively steady haze level throughout a track — sudden haze changes feel jarring and break the emotional flow

## Venue Scale Notes

Trance scales dramatically with venue size, and the genre's visual identity shifts accordingly:

### Festival Trance (ASOT-scale, thousands of attendees)
- **Laser dominance**: Massive laser arrays with full RGB coverage, parallel beams filling the sky
- **LED installations**: Large video walls, architectural LED integration
- **Movement scale**: Widest possible mover sweeps, all movers synchronized in dramatic formation changes
- **Pyrotechnics**: Timed to main releases (optional but common)
- **Haze**: Heavy haze to make laser arrays visible and create "cathedral" effect
- **Color impact**: Rely on saturation and brightness — colors must read from 100+ meters away
- **Peak technique**: "Wall of light" is the dominant release aesthetic — every fixture full for maximum overwhelming impact
- **Timing patience**: Extended 16-32 bar holds and sweeps feel appropriate at scale
- **Fixture count**: Dozens of movers, multiple laser systems, comprehensive LED coverage

### Club Trance (1000 attendees or fewer)
- **Intimate scale**: Smaller mover sweeps (4-8 bar arcs instead of 12-16 bar), focus on quality over quantity
- **Laser restraint**: Minimal laser use compared to festival — 1-2 laser systems maximum, clean geometric patterns
- **Movers as focus**: Fewer movers (4-8) but precise positioning and movement
- **Haze subtlety**: Medium haze, enough for beam visibility but not oppressive
- **Color depth**: Rely on color transitions and emotional journey rather than overwhelming brightness
- **Peak technique**: "Unified wash" rather than "wall of light" — emphasis on beauty and flow
- **Breakdown intimacy**: Extended single-color holds create vulnerability and emotional connection
- **Connection**: Lighting design supports DJ-audience connection, not spectacle distance

### Home Studio / Practice Space (small, intimate, 50-100 people)
- **Fixture constraints**: 2-4 movers max, minimal laser (single fixture), 4BAR and pars for base color
- **Movement subtlety**: Slow 4-8 bar transitions feel dramatic in intimate space
- **Haze minimal**: Light haze only during releases, avoid oppression
- **Color focus**: Emotional color journey is THE tool — saturation and timing matter more than quantity
- **Breakdown as centerpiece**: Long single-color holds (even longer than club) for maximum intimacy
- **Par wash**: Often the primary release effect (all pars unified color), with mover accents
- **Simplicity**: Fewer layers, more hold time per scene, careful timing spacing

## Notable References

### Lighting Design Pioneers
- **André Beekmans** (A State Of Trance LD): The definitive reference for festival trance lighting. His philosophy: "The whole room should become the light show, not just the stage." Signature techniques include laser cathedral arrays, room-filling haze, converged mover formations during breakdowns, and the "wall of light" release aesthetic. Study ASOT livestreams from 2015-2020 for textbook trance lighting.

### Key Trance Lighting Moments
- **The Breakdown Hold**: Trance breaks every 32-64 bars for the emotional breakdown. The lighting holds a single color, often in converged movers + unified par wash. This 16+ bar stillness is what makes the next release feel transcendent. Patience is the technique.
- **Color Evolution Arc**: Intro (deep blue) → Build 1 (blue/purple) → Release (bright cyan/white) → Breakdown (blue or pink) → Main Build (purple/magenta) → Main Release (white/magenta). This journey mirrors the emotional arc.
- **Laser Cathedral Effect**: Parallel vertical laser beams (full RGB) filling the entire room from stage to ceiling, ideally extending into audience space. This is the visual signature of festival trance peaks.
- **Haze + Beam Synergy**: Trance was the genre that made haze a primary tool (not accent). Beams without haze are invisible; haze without beams is just fog. Together, they create the "temple" or "cathedral" feeling unique to trance.

### Audio-Visual Alignment
- **Melodic phrasing**: Mover sweeps should match the vocal or lead synth phrasing — long, legato lines feel trance-appropriate; choppy, staccato movements feel off
- **Build structure**: Layer fixtures every 4-8 bars, matching the track's harmonic rhythm
- **Release moment**: The drop (or return of the main beat after breakdown) should trigger simultaneously:
  - All movers widen to full sweep
  - Pars hit maximum brightness
  - Lasers ignite at full RGB spread
  - Haze settles in for beam visibility
- **Breakdown entrance**: Happens over 1-2 bars (sharp but not jarring) as movers converge and color singularizes

### Festival vs. Club Differentiation
- **Luminosity Beach Festival**: Trance's most visual showcase — massive stages, laser arrays, LED walls, thousands of attendees. Expect "wall of light" releases and extended haze/laser combinations.
- **ASOT (club editions)**: Smaller venues, intimate breakdowns, focused lighting that supports DJ presence. Emphasis on emotional journey over spectacle.
- **Dreamstate Trance Festival**: Another major reference for outdoor scale — massive laser coverage, architectural lighting integration, sustained coverage of audience space.

## Key Philosophy

Trance lighting is about **emotional journey and euphoria**. Every lighting choice should
serve the emotional arc — from delicate beauty through tension to cathartic release. The
breakdown is the most important section (not the drop) — treat it with intimacy and patience.
Patience IS the trance lighting technique: hold the single-color breakdown for the full 16-32 bars,
let the tension build slowly, and then release with overwhelming brightness and laser arrays.
Use the longest timing presets, sweeping movements that feel soaring, and color evolution that
mirrors the track's emotional phrasing. The goal is to make the audience FEEL something
transcendent, not just see something. Trance is the genre where "less is more" for effects but
"more is more" for emotion — and the breakdown is where that philosophy lives.
