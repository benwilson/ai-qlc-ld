# Mood: Chaotic

## Overview

Unpredictable, wild, sensory overload. Rules go out the window — every fixture doing
something different, colors clashing, movements unsynchronized, effects stacked on effects.
The audience should feel overwhelmed in the best possible way. Think final boss fight,
the last 30 seconds before midnight on NYE, pure unfiltered madness.

Use when: the absolute peak of the peak, b2b DJ moments, crowd-goes-wild moments, the
one moment per set where you throw everything at the wall. Use SPARINGLY — chaos only
works as contrast to control.

## Category

emotional

## Visual References

- A pinball machine in tilt mode — every light firing independently
- NYE countdown at Times Square — sensory overload from every direction
- A rave in a hall of mirrors — infinite reflections of clashing colors

## Color Scheme

- **Type**: split-complementary (or none)
- **Rationale**: Chaotic mood deliberately breaks color scheme rules. Use multiple clashing
  schemes simultaneously — that's the point. If forced to pick, split-complementary provides
  maximum variety with some structure.

## Color Filtering

- **Temperature bias**: None. All colors are on the table. Temperature shifts rapidly.
- **Saturation preference**: Maximum. Fully saturated, clashing colors. No subtlety.
- **Palette selection**: Ignore the genre's two-color pairs. Instead, use ALL colors from
  the genre palette simultaneously or in rapid succession. If the genre has 6 colors,
  cycle through all 6.
- **Preferred colors**: All of them. Red next to blue next to green. Clashing combinations
  that normally wouldn't pair together. Different colors on different fixtures.
- **Max simultaneous colors**: No limit. Every fixture can be a different color. The more
  colors at once, the more chaotic.
- **Par behavior override**: Each par a different color, changing independently. No
  `par_wash()` — use `fourbar()` with four different colors. Missyees different from 4BAR.
  No coordination.

## Intensity Modifiers

- **Base brightness**: 100% of genre default. Full blast.
- **Contrast ratio**: Extreme but random. Jump between blackout and full, but not in a
  predictable pattern. Random-feeling contrast.
- **Blackout frequency**: Medium-high. Brief blackouts (1/2 beat) as punctuation, but
  unpredictable timing.
- **Number of fixtures active**: Constantly changing. 1 fixture one beat, all 7 the next,
  3 the next. No consistent count.
- **Fixture density**: Maximum — every fixture active, no coordination between them. All fixtures
  firing at the same time creates sensory overload.
- **Dimmer behavior**: Either full on or full off per fixture. No intermediate values.
  Binary intensity creates the sharpest chaos.
- **Key-to-fill ratio**: 1:1 — no focal point, no hierarchy, everything equally lit. Equal
  intensity distribution eliminates visual anchors and increases disorientation.

## Movement Modifiers

- **Speed multiplier**: 2x genre default. Fast, snappy movements. Movers should NOT follow predictable
  patterns — each fixture at its own speed.
- **Width preference**: Maximum, but varying. Some fixtures tight, others wide,
  simultaneously. Different fixtures at different positions to eliminate visual coordination.
- **Movement style**: Angular, random, unsynchronized. Movers should NEVER move together — this is
  critical for chaos. Sharpy goes left while BSW goes right while Profile snaps up. Each fixture
  on its own trajectory.
- **Position hold time**: Minimum. Positions change every 1–2 beats. Zero predictability.
- **Preferred positions**: All positions, rapidly cycling. Include Audience Blinder in
  the rotation. Cross patterns where fixtures swap sides. Random position selection for maximum
  disorientation.

## Effect Density

- **Strobe**: Heavy and varied. Different strobe speeds on different fixtures simultaneously.
  Sharpy at fast strobe while BSW is at slow strobe. Strobe appearing and disappearing
  unpredictably.
- **Lasers**: Full. All NI3K lasers on, laser strobe, maximum visual density.
- **Gobos**: Yes, changing. Different gobos on different fixtures. Gobo rotation at maximum.
- **Prism**: Yes, both. Dual prism spinning at high speed on all movers. Maximum beam
  multiplication.
- **Frost**: Vary it. Some fixtures frosted, others sharp. Mix beam types for visual texture.
- **Haze**: Maximum — chaos is enhanced by poor visibility and disorientation. Thick haze creates
  a solid volume of light where individual beams blur together. The room becomes a fog of
  unpredictable brightness.
- **Desynchronization requirement**: CRITICAL — fixtures must NOT be in sync. Program randomized
  timing offsets so strobes, gobos, and laser effects all trigger at different moments. Use different
  chase speeds on each mover to create polyrhythmic visual confusion.

## Timing Modifiers

- **Fade speed**: 0 (instant). Everything snaps. No fades in chaos.
- **Exception**: None. Every change is instant.
- **Hold duration**: Minimum possible. 1/4 beat or 1/2 beat holds.
- **Chase speed**: 2–3x genre default. Fastest possible chases. Multiple chases running
  simultaneously at different speeds for polyrhythmic visual chaos.

## Energy Curve

- **Baseline**: Peak. Chaotic mood starts at maximum and stays there.
- **Peak scaling**: 150% of genre peak. Beyond maximum — every effect stacked.
- **Rise time**: Instant. Chaos arrives fully formed.
- **Fall time**: Instant. Chaos ends with a hard blackout or snap to a controlled look.
- **Sustain**: Short. Chaos should last 8–16 bars MAXIMUM — research confirms this is the optimal
  window. Beyond that, the audience becomes numb to it and chaos loses all impact. Think of chaotic
  mood as a "panic button" — use it sparingly, only at the absolute peak moment, then return to
  genre conventions immediately. Overuse destroys the effect.

## Genre Interaction Notes

- **CRITICAL WARNING**: Chaotic mood should be used for 8–16 bars maximum, then return to the genre's
  normal conventions. Sustained chaos destroys all impact and audience fatigue sets in. Think of it as
  a "panic button" for the absolute peak moment only. One chaotic section per set is ideal. More than
  two in a single set diminishes the effect significantly.
- **DnB**: Drop 2, final 8 bars. Everything unleashed before the track ends. Then snap to
  blackout for the mix transition. Perfect moment for chaotic because it's the expected climax.
- **Dubstep**: The biggest drop, final wobble section. Maximum visual overload matching the
  sonic overload. Chaotic mode for 8–12 bars, then cutoff to silence.
- **House**: Very rare in house. Only at the peak of a festival mainstage set during the absolute
  high-energy moment. Use with extreme caution — house audiences expect control. 8 bars maximum.
- **Techno**: Almost never. Chaos is the opposite of techno's values. Only for the most
  extreme hard techno/industrial moments, and then only 4–8 bars. Return to minimalism immediately.
- **Trance**: The final release after the main breakdown, for the first 8 bars only. Then
  settle into euphoric. The chaos IS the catharsis — use this moment deliberately for the emotional
  payoff.
- **Melodic Techno**: Extremely rare. Only for the climactic moment of a 10+ minute track. 4–8 bars
  maximum before returning to the genre's precise, controlled aesthetic.
- **Psytrance**: Possible for the final peak. 8–12 bars of chaotic intensity creates the ultimate
  peak-floor experience. Use desynchronized strobes + lasers for psychedelic disorientation.
- **Liquid DnB**: Contradictory pairing but possible for a jarring climactic moment. 6–8 bars only.
  Violates the genre's smoothness deliberately, then return to liquid immediately. Impact through
  contrast.
- **Future Bass**: Occasional use during the climactic moment. 8–10 bars of chaotic neon creates
  sensory overload. Requires immediate reset to controlled drops to avoid overwhelming the audience.
- **Hardstyle**: Rare but intense when used. The moment after the climax reaches peak. 8 bars of
  maximum strobing, laser fire, and fixture density. Must snap to darkness for impact.
