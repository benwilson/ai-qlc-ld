# Mood: Dark

## Overview

Menacing, oppressive, shadow-heavy. Darkness is the primary design element — light is used
sparingly and deliberately. Every beam that cuts through the dark carries weight. The room
should feel like it's pressing in on you. Think underground warehouse, Berghain at 4am,
the moment before a horror movie jump scare.

Use when: dark techno, neurofunk DnB, heavy dubstep, industrial sets, or any moment where
you want the audience to feel uneasy tension.

## Category

emotional

## Visual References

- Berghain at 4am — a single red beam cutting through industrial haze
- The opening of a horror film — near-darkness, one light source, something lurking
- A solar eclipse — the world going dark with a narrow ring of blinding light

## Color Scheme

- **Type**: monochromatic
- **Rationale**: Dark mood uses one color at a time against blackness — the isolation of
  a single hue creates oppressive focus and eliminates visual comfort.

## Color Filtering

- **Temperature bias**: Cold. Strongly prefer cool end of the genre palette.
- **Saturation preference**: Desaturated or deeply saturated — no mid-range. Either near-black
  or a single intense color punching through darkness.
- **Palette selection**: From the genre's two-color pairs, prefer any pair that includes
  black/off as one color. If none exist, use the darkest pair and reduce intensity of the
  "lighter" color by 50%.
- **Preferred colors**: Deep reds, dark purples, cold blues, white (only for brief piercing
  moments). Avoid warm colors entirely.
- **Max simultaneous colors**: 1. Dark mood means one color at a time, maximum. The rest is
  shadow.
- **Par behavior override**: Prefer pars OFF or at very low intensity (< 30%). Pars create
  ambient fill which fights the darkness. Let movers do the work as isolated beams.

## Intensity Modifiers

- **Base brightness**: 30% of genre default. The room should be mostly dark.
- **Contrast ratio**: Maximum. The difference between "dark" and "bright" moments should be
  the largest possible — near-blackout to full single beam.
- **Blackout frequency**: High. Use blackouts between sections and as a rhythmic tool. Brief
  blackouts (1–2 beats) within sections for tension.
- **Number of fixtures active**: Minimum. 1–2 fixtures at a time during low energy, 3–4 at
  peak. Never all fixtures simultaneously except for a brief strobe burst.
- **Fixture density**: Sparse — 1–2 fixtures at a time during low energy, 3–4 max at peak.
  Sparse density maintains isolation and oppressive atmosphere.
- **Dimmer behavior**: Keep most fixture dimmers at 50–70% even during "bright" moments.
  Full intensity only for strobes and single-beat impacts.
- **Key-to-fill ratio**: 10:1 or higher — one dominant light source, almost no fill. The stark
  ratio reinforces isolation and menace.

## Movement Modifiers

- **Speed multiplier**: 0.7x genre default. Slower movements feel heavier and more ominous.
- **Width preference**: Narrow. Movers stay close to center or converge to a single point.
  Avoid wide spreads — they make the room feel open, not oppressive.
- **Movement style**: Angular and deliberate. Sharp position changes with holds, not smooth
  curves. Movers should feel like they're stalking, not dancing.
- **Position hold time**: 2x genre default. Let positions linger — the stillness builds tension.
- **Preferred positions**: Center, DJ Booth, Ceiling Hit. Avoid Audience Blinder (too
  aggressive for the mood's restrained menace) and wide positions (DSL, DSR).

## Effect Density

- **Strobe**: Rare. Reserve for single-beat impacts at the most critical moments. When used,
  white strobe only — color strobe is too "pretty" for dark mood.
- **Lasers**: Minimal. Single laser color (red preferred) as a static beam cutting through
  haze. No laser strobe. Lasers as architecture, not effects.
- **Gobos**: Yes. Tight, angular gobos create shadows and texture. Use static gobos (no
  rotation) for an ominous, frozen quality.
- **Prism**: Rarely. Single prism only, no rotation. Creates multiplied beams for brief
  moments of visual complexity.
- **Frost**: Avoid. Soft/frosted beams are the opposite of what Dark mood wants. Hard, tight
  beams cutting through shadow.
- **Haze**: Heavy — darkness needs visible beams cutting through thick haze to create oppressive
  atmosphere. Without haze, the isolated beams lose their impact. Essential to Dark mood's design.

## Timing Modifiers

- **Fade speed**: 1.5x genre default (slower fades). Lights should creep in and out of
  existence, not snap.
- **Exception**: Drop/impact moments still use instant snaps — the slow fade makes the
  sudden snap more shocking by contrast.
- **Hold duration**: 2x genre default. Let looks breathe in the darkness.
- **Chase speed**: 0.5x genre default. Any chases should be slow and deliberate.

## Energy Curve

- **Baseline**: Low. Dark mood sustains a low energy floor even during "high" genre sections.
- **Peak scaling**: 70% of genre peak. The "loudest" moment in Dark mood is still restrained
  compared to the genre's unmodified peak.
- **Rise time**: Slow. Energy builds gradually — don't jump from low to high.
- **Fall time**: Instant. Energy drops are sudden — snap to darkness.
- **Sustain**: Long. Dark mood prefers long sustained states over rapid changes.

## Phases (Optional)

### Phase 1: Stillness
- **Duration**: 8–16 bars or until operator transitions
- **Character**: Near-total darkness. A single fixture at low intensity. The room holds its breath.
- **Modifier adjustments**: Base brightness 15%, only 1 fixture active, no movement, no effects.

### Phase 2: Stalking
- **Duration**: 8–16 bars
- **Character**: Light begins to move — slowly, deliberately, like something hunting.
- **Modifier adjustments**: Base brightness 25%, add second fixture, slow angular movement begins,
  optional static gobo for shadow texture.

### Phase 3: Strike
- **Duration**: 2–4 bars
- **Character**: Brief, violent burst of light before returning to darkness.
- **Modifier adjustments**: Base brightness 100% for 1–2 beats, white strobe hit, then instant
  snap back to Phase 1 levels. The contrast is the point.

## Genre Interaction Notes

- **DnB**: Dark DnB (neurofunk, dark step) is a perfect match. Reduce drop intensity to 70%,
  extend breakdown darkness, use single blue or red beam during rolling sections.
- **Dubstep**: Already a dark genre — Dark mood pushes it further. Near-total blackout in
  breakdowns, single fixture during builds, reduced strobe on drops.
- **Techno**: Natural pairing. Techno's minimal aesthetic + Dark mood = Berghain. Use the
  techno palette (white, deep red) at even lower intensity. Hold static looks even longer.
- **House**: Unusual combination but effective for deep house. Strip the warm palette to just
  blues and teals. Reduce par usage dramatically. Movers only.
- **Trance**: Challenging pairing — trance's euphoria fights Dark mood. Use for psytrance or
  dark progressive. Favor purples and blues, suppress the bright/warm palette entirely.
- **Melodic Techno**: Excellent match — Dark mood enhances the Afterlife aesthetic. Push even
  darker, use cold blue + red only, minimal movement during breakdowns, single beam piercing moments.
- **Psytrance**: Particularly effective for dark psy variants. Favor green + purple at low
  intensity, static gobos for hypnotic patterns, slow angular movement creates haunting quality.
- **Liquid DnB**: Challenging but rewarding. Suppress warmth entirely, favor cold blues + teals,
  reduce fixture density even lower, use slower fades to preserve the genre's liquid movement.
- **Future Bass**: Unusual pairing but possible for "dark future bass" aesthetic. Mute the neon
  saturation, use deep magentas + dark purples, slower position changes than the genre suggests.
- **Hardstyle**: Excellent for raw hardstyle moments. Red + white + near-blackout creates visceral
  impact, strobe sparingly on climactic moments, intensity contrast at maximum for drop violence.
