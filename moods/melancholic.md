# Mood: Melancholic

## Overview

Sad, reflective, muted, contemplative. Light should feel like memory — faded, soft,
slightly distant. The audience should feel a beautiful ache, a bittersweet quality.
Think rain on a window, empty rooms, late-night solitude, the quiet after everyone
has left.

Use when: emotional breakdowns, sad or reflective tracks, transitions between sets,
ambient interludes, late-night deep sets.

## Category

emotional

## Visual References

- Rain on a window at night — blurred city lights through water droplets
- An empty theatre after everyone has left — a single ghost light on stage
- A fading photograph — colors washed out, soft, nostalgic

## Color Scheme

- **Type**: analogous
- **Rationale**: Adjacent muted colors (blues→purples or blue→teal) create a harmonious,
  subdued palette that feels unified in its sadness.

## Color Filtering

- **Temperature bias**: Cool-to-cold (default) or warm-desaturated (nostalgic variant). Blues,
  purples, and desaturated tones for the primary melancholic palette. Warm desaturated
  ambers create an alternative "nostalgic" feel — still sad but wistful rather than lonely.
- **Saturation preference**: Low. Muted, washed-out colors. Desaturate everything — add
  white to cool down and fade the palette. For RGB fixtures, never use full 255 on any
  single channel — cap at 180 and mix channels.
- **Palette selection**: From the genre's two-color pairs, prefer pairs with blue, purple,
  or teal. Specifically prefer the "moody" or "atmospheric" pairs. Avoid pairs with red,
  orange, or white (too intense/warm).
- **Preferred colors**: Muted blue (100, 120, 200), lavender (140, 100, 200), soft teal
  (80, 180, 160), desaturated purple (100, 60, 160), cold grey-white (160, 170, 180).
  Nostalgic variant: warm desaturated ambers (140, 100, 50), soft honey (160, 130, 60),
  faded gold (130, 110, 40).
- **Max simultaneous colors**: 1–2. Monochromatic or analogous color schemes only. The
  palette should feel narrow and unified.
- **Par behavior override**: Pars at low intensity (30–40%) in desaturated blues, purples,
  or warm ambers. `par_wash()` only — no chases, no alternating patterns. Uniform, soft,
  enveloping.

## Intensity Modifiers

- **Base brightness**: 35% of genre default. Dim but visible. The room should feel dusky.
- **Contrast ratio**: Very low. The difference between "bright" and "dark" moments is subtle.
  Everything stays in a narrow intensity band.
- **Blackout frequency**: None. Blackouts are too dramatic for melancholic mood. Instead,
  fade to near-darkness (5–10% single color).
- **Number of fixtures active**: Low-medium. 2–3 fixtures creating a soft, limited pool of
  light. Not all fixtures — that's too "full" feeling.
- **Fixture density**: Low. Fewer fixtures create a sense of isolation and loneliness. Selective
  placement (center stage, back wall) leaves large areas of darkness that feel contemplative
  rather than oppressive.
- **Dimmer behavior**: 20–50% range. Never full brightness. Even "bright" moments feel
  subdued.
- **Key-to-fill ratio**: 5:1. Strong directional key light with minimal fill. Sharp shadows
  and a clear sense of light coming from a single source — like a spotlight on an empty stage
  or lamplight in a darkened room.

## Movement Modifiers

- **Speed multiplier**: 0.3x genre default. Very slow, almost imperceptible movement.
- **Width preference**: Narrow to medium. Movers stay close together or at center. The
  light should feel like it's retreating inward, not reaching out.
- **Movement style**: Slow, smooth, slightly drooping. Movements that feel like a sigh —
  gradual downward tilts, slow convergence. Avoid upward movements (too hopeful) and
  wide spreads (too open).
- **Position hold time**: 3x genre default. Long, lingering holds. The light sits and thinks.
- **Preferred positions**: Center (contemplation), DJ Booth (solitude), Back Wall Wash
  (backdrop). Avoid Audience Blinder (too aggressive) and full sweeps (too energetic).

## Effect Density

- **Strobe**: None. Absolutely no strobe. Strobe is the opposite of melancholy.
- **Lasers**: None. Lasers are too "exciting" for this mood.
- **Gobos**: Optional. A soft, organic gobo pattern held static creates beautiful
  shadow-play that feels nostalgic. No rotation.
- **Prism**: None. Prism creates visual complexity and energy — wrong for melancholic.
- **Frost**: Yes. Heavy frost on all movers to soften beams into gentle pools of light.
  Hard beams feel too assertive for this mood.
- **Haze**: Medium. Enough to soften beams and diffuse light like rain on a window, but
  not so heavy it feels oppressive or dark. The haze should make light feel gentle and
  distant, not mysterious or menacing (which would be Dark mood).

## Timing Modifiers

- **Fade speed**: 4x genre default (very slow fades). Colors drift in and out like thoughts.
  A color change should take 8–16 bars.
- **Exception**: None. Everything moves slowly.
- **Hold duration**: 3x genre default. Long, meditative holds.
- **Chase speed**: 0.25x genre default. Any chase should be barely perceptible.

## Energy Curve

- **Baseline**: Low. Melancholic mood stays low and quiet.
- **Peak scaling**: 40% of genre peak. Even the "biggest" moments are restrained and muted.
- **Rise time**: Very slow. Energy barely rises at all.
- **Fall time**: Very slow. Energy fades gently.
- **Sustain**: Maximum. Melancholic mood sustains its low, steady state. Changes are rare
  and subtle.

## Genre Interaction Notes

- **DnB**: Works for atmospheric and liquid DnB. Completely suppress drop behavior — treat
  everything as a rolling atmospheric section. Muted blues and purples.
- **Dubstep**: Melodic dubstep or chillstep only. Suppress all aggressive conventions.
  Treat the track as a slow, sad wash.
- **Future Bass**: Good match for melancholic future bass tracks. Many future bass songs
  have introspective, emotional moments with vulnerable vocals. Use soft, warm ambers with
  minimal movement (nostalgic variant preferred). Keep pads glowing gently rather than
  dramatic.
- **Hardstyle**: Works for emotional hardstyle moments only. Melancholic hardstyle is rare
  but when it appears (melancholic intro, emotional bridge), use cool desaturated blues
  with very slow movement. Suppress all aggressive conventions.
- **House**: Deep house at its most introspective. Shift house's warm palette to muted
  blues and purples. Reduce everything to its quietest.
- **Liquid DnB**: Excellent for emotional breakdowns. Liquid DnB often has vulnerable,
  melancholic moments with rich soundscapes. Use soft, warm ambers (nostalgic variant)
  or cool blues depending on the vocal/emotional content. Long, gentle fades between colors.
- **Melodic Techno**: Excellent match. Much melodic techno has an inherently melancholic
  quality — introspective, reflective, late-night driven. Desaturate the palette, use
  minimal fixture density, and let slow fades create meditative moments. Perfect for
  extended ambient passages.
- **Psytrance**: Poor match. Psytrance's built-in energy and complexity fights melancholic
  restraint. Only use for psychedelic comedown sections or trippy, introspective moments
  (rare). Generally avoid this pairing.
- **Techno**: Works for ambient and dub techno. Already minimal — melancholic just makes it
  sadder. Desaturate the palette, slow everything further.
- **Trance**: Excellent for extended breakdowns. The breakdown IS the melancholic moment —
  single color, slow fade, intimate and reflective before the release.
